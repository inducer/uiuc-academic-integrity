* Presentation

** Academic Integrity: Why?

- /Nobody/ likes grades

  But they're not going away anytime soon
- Good grades can confer meaningful advantages in life
- Getting good grades takes work\dots

  \dots especially in hard/prestigious classes

  (``looks good on a transcript'')
- So people will cheat, or try to

\bigskip
Left unchecked, this would

- devalue transcripts and degrees
- demotivate honest participants
- demotivate instructors

** Infractions: Overview
The Big Four:

- Cheating
- Plagiarism
- Fabrication
- Facilitation

\medskip
Others:

- Bribes, Favors, Threats
- Academic Interference
** Infractions: Cheating

*Cheating*: use unauthorized resources or materials

- Let others work on your behalf
  - peer takes exam as you
  - hire a term paper company
- Submit the same work twice
- Collaborate when not permitted
- Let ChatGPT do the thing

** Infractions: Plagiarism

*Plagiarism:* Represent the words, work, or ideas of another as one's own
- Copying someone else's answer
- Quotation without disclosure (i.e. improper citation)
  - Copy from Github
  - Copy from a book
- Paraphrase without acknowledgment
  - Reword Wikipedia

\bigskip
*Proper Citation* makes all the difference:
#+BEGIN_QUOTE
If you explicitly and precisely disclose the source *in a place the instructor will notice*, you generally will not be accused of plagiarism.
#+END_QUOTE

** Infractions: Fabrication

*Fabrication:* Falsify, lie, or invent information
- Fake data in a lab experiment
- Alter an exam after grading
- Provide false or misleading information

** Infractions: Facilitation

*Facilitation:* Help (or attempt to help!) others commit infractions
- Allow another to copy
- Take an exam for someone else
- Grab a copy of an exam to show around

\bigskip
Sources and sinks:
- Often difficult to determine who copied and who allowed copying to happen
- Approach in student code: same treatment for ``source'' and ``sink''

** Clif Notes for the Remainder

#+LATEX: \begin{center}
\Huge *Just don't.*
#+LATEX: \end{center}

\bigskip
- Getting caught up in `the system' is *unpleasant*
  - Purposefully so
- /Will/ limit your ability to get awards/fellowships/\dots
- May or may not be reflected on your transcript
- Repeat offenses may get you kicked out of school

** Process

- Instructor `suspects something' or conducts routine check (e.g. automated plagiarism checking)
- Assembles body of evidence
- Files allegation via FAIR
  - Automated email to you, with a *deadline of 10 business days*
  - No recourse if you don't respond
  - *You are responsible for checking your Illinois email* sufficiently often
- Instructor may amend allegation during this time
- *Student response* via FAIR (not email or other way), may provide additional evidence
- Instructor files *finding* and sets *sanction*
- Student may appeal

** Sanctions
*Typical* (suggested by [[https://siebelschool.illinois.edu/academics/honor-code][CS Honor Code]]): Both
- *Zero* on assignment, /and/
- *Letter grade drop* in course

\medskip
Other options:
- Reduced grade on assignment/exam/evaluated work
- Denial of credit in proficiency exam
- Failing grade in course

\medskip
Uncommon:
- Required attendance at a noncredit AI (\dots) workshop
- Difficult make-up assignments
- Any sanction, discussed and agreed to in writing by instructor and student
- Written warning

** Appeals

Within *five business days of finding*: send email (FAIR has instructions).
Otherwise findings becomes final.

\bigskip
Can appeal either
- *finding and sanction*, or
- *just sanction*.

\bigskip
Need *grounds for appeal*. One of:
- (1) Instructor did not follow procedure, *and* this affected the outcome.
- (2) Instructor (or other official) had conflict of interest or bias that affected the outcome.
- (3) Sanctions were not appropriate for violation.
- (4) New information became available after instructor decision, *and* would have affected the outcome.

** Appeals Process: Committee

Required:

- Chair (CS faculty member, does not vote, impartial, writes report)
- Two voting CS faculty members
- One voting student member

\medskip
Optional:
- Appellant (may attend, hearing proceeds if not present)
- Instructor (may attend, hearing proceeds if not present)
- Consultant (at the appellant's option, may speak with appellant but no-one else)

** Appeals Process: Hearing

- *Appellant notified of time* five business days ahead of time, takes place on Zoom.
- Opening Statements by appellant and instructor
- *Open discussion*, led by voting committee members
  - Instructor and student may ask questions through chair
- Instructor, appellant, consultant dismissed
- *Closed discussion* and *vote* (simple majority)
- Chair writes report, department reviews and sends decision notice
